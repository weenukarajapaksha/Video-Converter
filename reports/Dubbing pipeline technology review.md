# Front-end models lag, back-end design leads

The pipeline's architecture is current for October 2026. Its weakest parts are the three "listening" stages that come before translation. Whisper large-v3 makes roughly **twice the Mandarin errors** of the 2026 Chinese-specialised open models on web-style audio. pyannote 3.1 is two releases behind. htdemucs_ft scores about **2.5 dB lower on vocal SDR** than the open RoFormer separators. The "speaking" half of the pipeline is in better shape. Gemini Flash translation with full context, a critic and an LLM rewrite step follows the 2024-2025 research pattern closely. Gemini TTS ranks **#2 on the Artificial Analysis speech arena** at a fraction of the leader's price. Wav2Lip-GAN is still the only lip-sync model that is both fast and accurate on 4 GB. Per stage, the verdicts are: separation **upgrade recommended** (cheap, drop-in); ASR **upgrade recommended** (largest accuracy gain); diarization **upgrade recommended** (cheapest fix); translation **good**, with length control that should move from words to syllables plus a choice among variants; TTS **optimal for the budget**; time-fitting **good** and in line with the literature; lip sync **good**, the right choice for 4 GB. No open end-to-end speech-to-speech model handles Mandarin-to-English dubbing with the timing, voice and licensing control this project needs. That makes the modular cascade the defensible design, not a legacy compromise. For a 5-day assessment, the best returns come from an evaluation harness, speaker-count constraints, a Qwen3-ASR + forced-aligner swap, and syllable-based length variants. None of these needs more than 4 GB, and most take less than a day.

## The listening stages hold the biggest accuracy gains

### Separation: a ten-line swap buys about 2.5 dB

Demucs is no longer competitive for vocal extraction. On the MVSEP Multisong benchmark, **htdemucs_ft scores 8.33 dB vocal SDR**. Open RoFormer checkpoints score higher: Mel-Band RoFormer (Kimberley Jensen) 11.01 dB and BS-RoFormer viperx 10.87 dB. The newest MVSEP service model, BS-RoFormer 124-band ver. 2026.07, reaches 12.33 dB ([MVSEP algorithms](https://mvsep.com/en/algorithms); [ZFTurbo pretrained models](https://github.com/ZFTurbo/Music-Source-Separation-Training/blob/main/docs/pretrained_models.md)). The 2026.07 model appears to be available only through MVSEP's service, so the realistic local gain is about +2.5 dB, not +4 dB. The same leaderboard shows something odd about the current choice. htdemucs_ft beats plain htdemucs by **only 0.15 dB** on Multisong, despite running four models in sequence ([demucs issue 426](https://github.com/facebookresearch/demucs/issues/426)). On this project's own 58 s clip the gap was 2.3 dB (22.1 vs 19.8 dB), at 3.5x the runtime (18.4 s vs 5.2 s). The gain is clearly content-dependent, which is a reason to A/B test any replacement on the project's own clip rather than trust the leaderboard.

The practical replacement is `audio-separator[gpu]`. It is MIT-licensed, works with CUDA on Windows, and its default model is already a Mel-Band RoFormer. Its `--use_native_fp16` flag is verified for RoFormers, and an `--mdxc_overlap` setting matters a lot: models with overlap 4-8 run **4-6x more forward passes** ([python-audio-separator](https://github.com/nomadkaraoke/python-audio-separator)). The Kim Mel-Band RoFormer weights are MIT on Hugging Face ([KimberleyJSN/melbandroformer](https://huggingface.co/KimberleyJSN/melbandroformer)). Speed on the RTX 3050 is the main uncertainty. No published benchmark exists for RoFormers on a 4 GB NVIDIA card. Secondary guidance suggests chunks of about 2.5-3 s at 4 GB ([audio-separator docs](https://docsearch.algolia.com/mcp/docs/repo/nomadkaraoke/python-audio-separator)), while the viperx config defaults to about 8 s ([HF config](https://huggingface.co/Eddycrack864/Music-Source-Separation-Training/blob/main/model_bs_roformer_ep_317_sdr_12.9755.yaml)). A reasonable expectation is about 2-5x realtime with fp16 and overlap 2, which is similar to htdemucs_ft, but this is an inference and has not been measured. Separation takes about 10% of the 154 s end-to-end run, so the case for switching is quality, not speed. Keep plain htdemucs as the "fast" mode.

Film audio raises a further question that music leaderboards cannot answer. Music separators put any *singing* into the vocal stem and can leak sound effects. Cinematic dialogue/music/effects models such as Bandit, Banquet and MVSEP's DnR v3 SCNet/MelBand are trained to split dialogue from everything else, with DnR v3 data covering 30+ languages ([arXiv 2309.02539](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02539v3); [MVSEP algorithm 64](https://mvsep.com/algorithms/64)). They are small (Bandit has 37M parameters), but the open Bandit/Banquet weights are **strictly non-commercial** ([arXiv 2408.03588](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.03588v2)). No one has published a head-to-head of RoFormer and DnR models on real film dialogue. The DnR route is worth an A/B for building the music-and-effects bed, but it is not a confirmed improvement.

### ASR: Chinese-specialised models halve the error rate

On Mandarin, Whisper large-v3 is now a generation behind. Qwen's own table puts it at **9.86 / 19.11% CER on WenetSpeech net / meeting**, against 4.97 / 5.88% for Qwen3-ASR-1.7B and 5.97 / 6.88% for the 0.6B model. On dialect-heavy KeSpeech the gap is 28.79% vs 5.10% ([QwenLM/Qwen3-ASR](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-ASR)). FireRedASR2-AED, released February 2026, does better still on FireRed's own evaluation: a **3.05% average over four Mandarin sets**, against 3.76% for Qwen3-ASR-1.7B. It also outputs word timestamps and confidence scores and comes with a VAD that beats Silero on F1 (97.57 vs 95.95). However, it is tested only on Ubuntu, and the README says Windows is untested ([FireRedASR2S](https://github.com/FireRedTeam/FireRedASR2S)). These are vendor-reported numbers with different text normalisation, and none of them is a film or TV drama benchmark. WenetSpeech-net is the closest proxy. Still, the direction is consistent across the FireRed, Qwen and independent Qwen-Audio-3.1 tables ([arXiv 2610.28711](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.28711)).

Qwen3-ASR is the pragmatic choice because it fixes three of the project's observed problems together. Stronger Chinese language priors should reduce homophone errors such as 丹代/唐代. Forcing the language to zh removes the Japanese drift seen in batched Whisper. Segment-level decoding on VAD chunks under 30-40 s makes the coverage re-decode workaround mostly unnecessary. The companion **Qwen3-ForcedAligner-0.6B** reports an average timing shift of 42.9 ms, against 133.2 ms for WhisperX. On 300 s concatenated audio the gap is 52.9 ms vs 2,708 ms ([QwenLM/Qwen3-ASR](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-ASR)). That is the most direct fix for speaker boundaries landing "a syllable off". Both models are Apache-2.0 and install with `pip install qwen-asr`. A CPU int8 ONNX export of the 0.6B model with a Windows x64 example exists as a fallback ([sherpa-onnx](https://pub.dev/packages/sherpa_onnx_linux/versions/1.13.7/example)). VRAM is the main uncertainty. Estimates from parameter counts put the 0.6B model at about 1.2 GB in bf16, which is comfortable, and the 1.7B model at about 3.4 GB, which is too tight for a 4 GB laptop card that shares memory with the Windows display. No README publishes measured VRAM or real-time factor on consumer GPUs. The existing faster-whisper path, with its coverage self-check, should stay behind a config flag as the fallback.

On the cloud side, Chinese-native APIs lead a unified evaluation. Fun-ASR-Flash scores 3.83% and Tencent Hy-ASR 4.16% on WenetSpeech-net, while GPT-4o Transcribe scores 12.27% ([arXiv 2610.28711](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.28711)). Gemini 3.5 Transcribe is cheap (about $0.005/min) and offers word timestamps, diarization and a custom vocabulary of up to 1,000 terms. However, **no Chinese CER has been published for it** ([eesel.ai](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/gemini-3-5-transcribe)), and Gemini 2.5 Pro was weak on WenetSpeech (14.43%). Treat it as an experiment, not an upgrade.

### Diarization: the cheapest fix is a parameter, not a model

The over-splitting problem (five speakers found for about three) does not need a new model at first. Passing `min_speakers`/`max_speakers` and merging clusters whose centroids are very similar addresses it directly. After that, **pyannote community-1** (pyannote.audio 4.x, CC-BY-4.0) improves DER on almost every benchmark: AliMeeting 24.5 → 20.3, AISHELL-4 12.2 → 11.7, DIHARD3 21.4 → 20.2. It also adds an `exclusive_speaker_diarization` output with one speaker at a time, designed for aligning with transcript words ([HF community-1](https://huggingface.co/pyannote/speaker-diarization-community-1)). DiariZen-Large-v2 is far stronger on Mandarin meetings (AliMeeting **10.8** vs 24.4 for 3.1), but its weights are CC BY-NC and it pins torch 2.1.1 ([BUTSpeechFIT/DiariZen](https://github.com/BUTSpeechFIT/DiariZen)). That is acceptable for a student assessment but adds environment risk. The 4.x upgrade carries a Windows trap: TorchCodec supports only FFmpeg 4-7 and has to match the torch version. Passing in-memory waveforms avoids this ([HF forum](https://huggingface.co/datasets/John6666/forum2/blob/main/torchcodec_windows_error_1.md)). Even so, every system does badly on film-like audio. AVA-AVD (movies) DER is **49.7 / 44.6 / 37.1** for 3.1 / community-1 / the paid precision-2 model. Speaker attribution in film will still need the punctuation-aware boundary snapping the project already has, plus per-word assignment from a good aligner.

## The speaking stages are already near the frontier

### Translation: right pattern, imprecise length unit

Full-conversation context, an LLM critic and a rewrite of over-long lines already match the main LLM-MT pattern of 2024-2025. The gap is in *what* the budget counts. English word length varies widely, so a 2.6 words/sec cap is a noisy proxy for speaking time. Dubbing research has moved to speech-aware units. VideoDubber conditions tokens on predicted speech duration ([arXiv 2211.16934](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.16934)). The EMNLP 2025 Duration-based Translation framework predicts a target phoneme count from source duration and reports up to **24% relative improvement in speech overlap** with competitive COMET ([ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-demos.37.pdf)). X-Dubbing (October 2026) plans syllable counts in the translator ([arXiv 2610.32042](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.32042)). A second consistent finding is that **generating several lengths and then selecting one beats a single constrained generation**. Huawei's IWSLT 2023 system re-ranked candidates by time deviation ([IWSLT 2023](https://aclweb.org/anthology/2023.iwslt-1.9.pdf)). Length-aware beam search improved MOS by +0.34 to +0.65 ([arXiv 2506.00740](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.00740)). A 2025 study of LLM prompting found that models shorten reliably only when shown "extreme" examples, and that multi-output selection reached state-of-the-art length-quality trade-offs ([arXiv 2506.04855](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.04855)). For this project, that means asking for normal / short / very-short variants in the same JSON, counting syllables in code, and picking the longest variant that fits the measured slot. That would remove most of the sequential rewrite calls the batch rewrite currently makes.

The critic's holistic 1-10 score is the other weak point. MQM-style output (errors with category and severity) is more interpretable and tells the reviser what to fix ([GEMBA-MQM](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.13988v1); [MQM-APE](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2409.14335v2)). Using a judge from a different model family reduces self-preference bias. That last point is an inference from the LLM-as-judge literature and has not been tested on MT. For evaluation, WMT25 ranked systems with GEMBA-ESA, MetricX-24-Hybrid-XL, XCOMET-XL and CometKiwi-XL. It also warned that any metric used *inside* a system for selection inflates that system's scores ([arXiv 2508.14909](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.14909v2)). MetricX-24's reference-free mode is nearly as accurate as its reference mode (ja-zh segment accuracy 0.509 vs 0.528) and is Apache-2.0 ([MetricX-24 XL](https://huggingface.co/google/metricx-24-hybrid-xl-v2p6-bfloat16)). The XL size will not fit 4 GB, so the Large variant or CometKiwi on CPU is the realistic option.

On model choice, the evidence does not justify a swap. zh→en was **not a WMT25 general-task direction**, and no 2026 zh→en film-dialogue benchmark exists for Gemini 3.x, GPT-5.x or Claude 5.x. Gemini 2.5 Pro and GPT-4.1 led WMT25's automatic ranking among general LLMs, alongside the specialist Hunyuan-MT-7B ([arXiv 2508.14909](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.14909v2); [Slator](https://slator.com/wmt25-preliminary-results-gemini-2-5-pro-gpt-4-1-lead-ai-translation/)). Flash-tier prices of $0.75 / $3.75 per 1M tokens for `gemini-3.8-flash` make cost negligible per video ([Gemini pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)). One correction to the research notes: the `gemini-3.5-flash` fallback ID is valid. It is listed on this API key's models endpoint, though not on the public pricing page, and it answered real requests during test runs. The existing failover chain, circuit breaker and low-thinking drafts are sound as built.

### TTS and time-fitting: keep the stack, change the order of operations

The project already uses the right expressive engine. On the Artificial Analysis arena (25 Sept 2026), **Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS ranks #2 at Elo 1265** for about $16.5 per 1M characters (about $0.0135/min). Eleven v4 now leads at Elo 1319, but costs $80 per 1M characters ([digitalapplied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/best-text-to-speech-models-september-2026-ranked-priced); [AA leaderboard](https://artificialanalysis.ai/text-to-speech/leaderboard/controlled-voice)). Emotion tags are already passed into Gemini's style prompts, so expressiveness is covered. Two budget risks apply. The Gemini price **doubles on 1 January 2027**, and Gemini 3.8 Flash-Lite TTS (Elo 1239, about $0.009/min) is a near-equal fallback. Open models remain well behind: the best commercially usable open options score about 1020-1065 Elo. A local cloning backend only makes sense if preserving the speaker's timbre is a requirement. In that case **Chatterbox Multilingual** (about 3.5 GB VRAM, MIT, `exaggeration` emotion control, `cfg_weight=0` to limit accent transfer) is the 4 GB candidate ([NVIDIA ACE model card](https://docs.nvidia.com/ace-for-games/chatterbox-tts/nvigi-chatterbox-model-card.html)). IndexTTS-2 is ruled out on several counts: its advertised precise-duration mode is **"not yet enabled"** in the released code, third-party guides put it at 8 GB or more, and its license is bilibili's own, not OSI-open ([index-tts](https://github.com/index-tts/index-tts)). Gemini cloning requires recorded verbal consent, and any cloning mode should be opt-in.

Time-fitting by text rewrite followed by a capped stretch is what the literature supports. Amazon's study of human dubbing found that dub/source duration ratios track content length more than speaking rate, which favours changing words over speeding up audio ([TACL 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.12137)). No published threshold exists for acceptable speech stretching. Dubbing listening tests stay within 0.8-1.2x ([Dub-S2ST](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.20899)), and WSOLA MOS falls steadily as the factor moves away from 1.0 ([ESOLA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1801.06492)). The 1.25x cap is therefore at the edge of the tested range, not beyond it. Two cheap changes would help. First, set the TTS rate *before* synthesis (an SSML rate for Edge, a pace instruction for Gemini) so Rubber Band only corrects the last 5-10%. Second, use a soft cap of about 1.15x, relaxed for off-screen speakers ([arXiv 2204.02530](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.02530)). Research is moving to generating speech at the target duration. X-Dubbing fixes the TTS mel length to the source slot and beat a cascaded baseline on naturalness MOS (**3.99 vs 3.54**), but its UTMOS was below Hibiki's ([arXiv 2610.32042](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.32042)). F5-TTS's `fix_duration` is the open equivalent. Its VRAM on 4 GB has not been verified, and its weight license needs checking.

### Lip sync: Wav2Lip-GAN is still the 4 GB answer

Diffusion lip-sync models now clearly beat Wav2Lip on visual quality. In OmniSync's HDTF table, Wav2Lip has the second-worst FVD (543 vs 200 for OmniSync). It still holds the **second-best SyncNet LSE-C (7.63)**, behind only LatentSync (8.05) ([OmniSync](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.21448v2)). None of the stronger models fits this hardware. LatentSync 1.5 needs at least 8 GB and 1.6 needs at least 18 GB ([LatentSync](https://github.com/bytedance/LatentSync)), and OmniSync and KeySync are research-scale. MuseTalk 1.5 is the only documented 4 GB upgrade. Its code and weights are MIT, it ships Windows scripts, and it is better on FID/FVD. But its authors measured about **5 minutes for 8 s of video on an RTX 3050 Ti 4 GB** ([MuseTalk](https://github.com/TMElyralab/MuseTalk)), roughly 37 s per video-second against the 2.2 s measured for Wav2Lip on this machine, and its LSE-C is lower (6.89). SyncNet scores also depend on how the audio and video are encoded, so cross-paper comparisons are loose ([THEval CVPR 2026](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026F/supplemental/Quignon_THEval._Evaluation_Framework_CVPRF_2026_supplemental.pdf)). Wav2Lip's real liability is its license: "personal/research/non-commercial" only ([Wav2Lip](https://github.com/Rudrabha/Wav2Lip)). That is fine for an assessment but should be stated. For two-shots, Light-ASD (1.0M parameters, 94.1% mAP on AVA-ActiveSpeaker) is a cheap way to link diarized speakers to face tracks ([CVPR 2023](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/html/Liao_A_Light_Weight_Model_for_Active_Speaker_Detection_CVPR_2023_paper.html)).

## End-to-end systems trade away the control dubbing needs

The case for a cascade does not depend on habit. In October 2026 the open end-to-end options do not cover this task. Kyutai's Hibiki-Zero (3B, ICML 2026) translates only French, Spanish, Portuguese and German into English, and its model card lists CC BY-NC-SA ([HF model card](https://huggingface.co/kyutai/hibiki-zero-3b-pytorch-bf16)). Meta's SeamlessExpressive does cover Mandarin, but it is gated and non-commercial ([HF seamless-expressive](https://huggingface.co/facebook/seamless-expressive)). Neither offers glossary enforcement, per-speaker voice assignment or explicit duration control. TransVIP (NeurIPS 2024) states that "most end-to-end models struggle to outperform cascade models", and that isochrony control "is absent in the majority of existing S2ST systems" ([arXiv 2405.17809](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.17809v3)). The frontier is joint audio-visual generation, such as JUST-DUB-IT ([arXiv 2601.22143](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.22143v1)) and X-Dubbing's duration-locked TTS. These models are multi-billion-parameter and far beyond 4 GB. The strongest arguments *against* the cascade still hold. Errors compound across stages, prosody can be lost between ASR and TTS, and latency is batch-scale: **154 s for a 50.6 s clip** on this machine, about 3x realtime, already down from 575 s.

Commercial services are the same cascade idea packaged as a product. ElevenLabs Dubbing v1 costs about $0.33-0.50 per source minute and reportedly had no lip sync as of July 2026 ([ElevenLabs help](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/help-center/product/dubbing/how-much-does-dubbing-cost/llms.txt); [Puter](https://developer.puter.com/tutorials/elevenlabs-api-pricing/)). BytePlus charges $0.299/min with lip sync vs $0.112/min audio-only ([BytePlus](https://docs.byteplus.com/docs/byteplus-media-live/ai_dubbing)). YouTube's auto-dubbing lip sync was still an opt-in pilot in October 2026 ([Social Samosa](https://www.socialsamosa.com/news-2/youtube-expands-auto-dubbing-tests-lip-sync-11077978)). Most of these prices come from third-party trackers and conflict in places. The useful comparison is not on price, since this pipeline's API cost is cents per video. It is that these services give no control over individual segments, and lip sync is usually a premium tier. Running one clip through ElevenLabs Dubbing v1 as an external baseline would be the cheapest way to back the "controllability vs convenience" argument with evidence.

## Conclusion

The research turns the usual assumption about this kind of pipeline upside down. The stages that look most "AI-heavy" (LLM translation, neural TTS, lip sync) are already near the practical frontier for a 4 GB laptop. The stages that look routine (separation, transcription, speaker labelling) are where 2026 models have moved furthest ahead. The errors these early stages make carry through every later stage, so a CER halved at the ASR stage is likely to improve translation, speaker voices and lip-sync timing more than any change to the translator. That claim is an inference and should be tested with the evaluation harness.

The second lesson is that the field's direction, generating text and speech *at* the target duration rather than repairing it afterwards, can be adopted at the prompt level today. Syllable budgets and choosing among length variants capture much of what X-Dubbing and DT gain from retraining, with no new model and no extra VRAM. The main uncertainties are that none of the recommended swaps has published VRAM or speed figures for an RTX 3050, and that no benchmark measures any of them on Mandarin film dialogue. Each one should therefore be adopted only after it wins an A/B test on the project's own clips.
